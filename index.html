Why Blanket Uplift Fails

A blanket national uplift fails because tournament demand is fundamentally local. The host city effect, fanbase demographics, and match stakes all create store-level demand patterns that a national model cannot see. Applying an average uplift to every store means over-stocking in cities with no host venue and no fanbase concentration — while leaving the stores that matter under-resourced. The $50M reallocation represents correcting exactly this misallocation before it happens.

Learning Loop Structure
Four structured loops — each triggered by a tournament milestone, each using new actuals to update assumptions and unlock incremental commitment
Loop Trigger Data Used What Updated Commitment Delta
Loop 1 Pre-tournament (T-4 wks) Analog calibration, ticket sales, hotel bookings City-tier demand multipliers, initial scenario widths +0% → 30% (initial commit)
Loop 2 After Group Stage Wk 1-2 actuals POS actuals vs. plan, watch-party conversion rates Food/bev uplift assumptions, daypart curve shape 30% → 52% (+22 pp)
Loop 3 Round of 16 complete City-level POS, team elimination outcomes Team affinity weights (adjust for eliminated teams), R16 performance 52% → 70% (+18 pp)
Loop 4 Quarterfinal complete SF/Final market data, remaining team fan concentration Final-week city allocations, apparel assortment for surviving teams 70% → 85% (+15 pp)
Why Staged Commitment Beats Full Pre-Commitment

No analog perfectly predicts a first-of-kind event. Committing 100% of inventory before the tournament would lock capital in scenarios that actuals quickly contradict — wrong cities, wrong teams still in, wrong SKU mix. The staged approach trades a small margin loss on expedited orders (loops 2–4) for a major reduction in markdown risk on committed-but-wrong inventory. Each loop narrows the scenario cone and unlocks the next tranche of commitment with much higher confidence.
